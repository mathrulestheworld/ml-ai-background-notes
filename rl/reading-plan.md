# Reading plan

## <a id="courses"></a>Courses

- **Main course — DeepMind x UCL Reinforcement Learning Lecture Series, 2021: Hado van Hasselt, Diana Borsa, and Matteo Hessel.** [Lecture playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLqYmG7hTraZDVH599EItlEWsUOsJbAodm). Thirteen lectures on the foundations: bandits, Markov decision processes and dynamic programming with their theory, model-free prediction and control, function approximation, planning and models, policy gradients, off-policy and multi-step learning, and deep reinforcement learning. The playlist is labeled as a deep-learning series on YouTube but holds the RL lectures. No official slides remain online; an [unofficial mirror](https://github.com/yjavaherian/deepmind-x-ucl-rl) collects the thirteen slide decks.
- **Main course — UCL, 2015: David Silver, *Introduction to Reinforcement Learning*.** [Course page with slides](https://davidstarsilver.wordpress.com/teaching/) · [Lecture playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLqYmG7hTraZDM-OYHWgPebj2MfCFzFObQ). The classic ten-lecture course, with slides for every lecture: MDPs, dynamic programming, model-free prediction and control, value function approximation, policy gradients, learning and planning, exploration, and games.
- **Main course — UC Berkeley CS285, Fall 2023: Sergey Levine, *Deep Reinforcement Learning*.** [Course page with slides](https://rail.eecs.berkeley.edu/deeprlcourse-fa23/) · [Lecture playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL_iWQOsE6TfVYGEGiAOMaOzzv41Jfm_Ps) · [Spring 2026 course](https://rail.eecs.berkeley.edu/deeprlcourse/) · [Spring 2026 playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLKq1TCpsv3Y4). The standard deep RL course: imitation, policy gradients, actor-critic, Q-learning, optimal control, model-based RL, exploration, offline RL, inverse RL, control as inference, and RL theory. Each Fall 2023 lecture is split into several videos; the Spring 2026 edition adds a lecture on RL for language models.
- **Supplement — Stanford CS234, Spring 2024: Emma Brunskill, *Reinforcement Learning*.** [Course page with slides](https://web.stanford.edu/class/archive/cs/cs234/cs234.1246/) · [Lecture playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rN4wG6Nk6sNpTEbuOSosZdX). Sixteen lectures, strongest on data-efficient RL: bandits and regret, Bayesian and PAC exploration, offline RL and policy evaluation, imitation and learning from preferences, and Monte Carlo tree search.
- **Supplement — Stanford CS224R, Spring 2025 and 2026: Chelsea Finn, *Deep Reinforcement Learning*.** [Course page with slides](https://cs224r.stanford.edu/) · [Spring 2025 playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLoROMvodv4rPwxE0ONYRa_itZFdaKCylL). Deep RL for robotics and language models: imitation, off-policy actor-critic, offline RL, reward learning, RL for language-model reasoning, multi-task and goal-conditioned RL, meta-RL, hierarchy, sim-to-real, and RL fine-tuning of vision–language–action models.
- **Supplement — Pieter Abbeel, *Foundations of Deep RL*, 2021.** [Lecture playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLwRJQ4m4UJjNymuBM9RdmB3Z9N5-0IlY0). Six compact lectures: exact methods and maximum-entropy RL, deep Q-learning, policy gradients and advantage estimation, TRPO and PPO, DDPG and SAC, and model-based RL.
- **Supplement for Part I — University of Alberta, *Reinforcement Learning Specialization*: Martha White and Adam White.** [Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/reinforcement-learning). Four short courses that follow Sutton and Barto closely: bandits, MDPs, and dynamic programming; Monte Carlo, TD, Q-learning, and Dyna; prediction and control with function approximation and policy gradients; and a capstone.
- **Supplement for control and partial observability — UC Berkeley CS287, Fall 2019: Pieter Abbeel, *Advanced Robotics*.** [Course page with slides and videos](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pabbeel/cs287-fa19/). Exact methods for MDPs, LQR and iterative LQR, trajectory optimization and model-predictive control, Bayes filters, POMDPs, imitation, and sim-to-real transfer.
- **Supplement for control — CMU 16-745, 2025: Zac Manchester, *Optimal Control and Reinforcement Learning*.** [Lectures and notes](https://optimalcontrol.ri.cmu.edu/lectures/) · [Lecture playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLZnJoM76RM6IAJfMXd1PgGNXn3dxhkVgI) · [Notebooks](https://github.com/Optimal-Control-16-745/lecture-notebooks). LQR from three viewpoints, convex MPC, differential dynamic programming, and direct trajectory optimization.
- **Supplements for theory — Harvard CS2824, Spring 2026: Kianté Brantley and Sham Kakade; Cornell CS6789, Fall 2024: Wen Sun; Csaba Szepesvári, CMPUT 653.** [CS2824 course page with slides](https://harvard-cs2824-s26.github.io/) · [CS6789 course page with slides](https://wensun.github.io/CS6789_fall_2024.html) · [CMPUT 653 lecture notes](https://rltheory.github.io/). Sample complexity, exploration, function approximation, policy optimization, and offline RL with proofs.
- **Supplement for language models — Nathan Lambert, *Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback*.** [Book](https://rlhfbook.com/) · [Course](https://rlhfbook.com/course). The book and lectures on post-training, with policy-gradient algorithms for language models and reasoning.

The module has two parts. **Part I** develops classical reinforcement learning in depth, from Markov decision processes to policy gradients, partial observability, and optimal control: the theory and algorithms that every later method builds on, with tabular and linear methods that can be analyzed exactly. **Part II** develops modern reinforcement learning with deep networks and follows it to the current frontier: deep value-based and actor-critic agents, exploration, world models, planning with learned models, imitation and offline RL, multi-agent RL, reinforcement learning for language models, generalist agents, the modern theory, and deployment. Each block lists the material to cover and its primary lectures; further reading and the labs provide additional depth. Timing is flexible; Part II assumes Part I.

Video links point to the lectures on YouTube where the individual video has been identified, and otherwise to the course playlist with the lecture's position. Several prerequisites come from earlier modules: expected utility and decision networks from AI chapter 12, game trees, minimax, and Monte Carlo tree search from AI chapter 4, equilibria and no-regret learning from AI chapter 15, filtering in hidden Markov models from AI chapter 11, stochastic gradient methods from DL chapter 3, and the score-function estimator from Generative AI chapter 3. Reinforcement learning from human feedback and for reasoning in language models is introduced in NLP chapters 11 and 12; block 28 develops its algorithms without repeating that material.

### <a id="reference-books-used-below"></a>Reference books used below

- **S&B:** Richard Sutton and Andrew Barto, [Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction](http://incompleteideas.net/book/the-book-2nd.html) (MIT Press, second edition, 2018). Free; the standard textbook, with a [full PDF](http://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf), and the backbone of Part I.
- **Murphy:** Kevin Murphy, [Reinforcement Learning: An Overview](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05265) (2024, updated 2025). Free; a compact survey of value-based, policy-based, and model-based RL, multi-agent RL, and RL for language models.
- **RLTA:** Agarwal, Brantley, Jiang, Kakade, and Sun, [Reinforcement Learning: Theory and Algorithms](https://rltheorybook.github.io/). Free; the 2026 draft of the book, with the chapter numbering used below; sample complexity, exploration, policy optimization, and imitation, with proofs.
- **LS:** Tor Lattimore and Csaba Szepesvári, [Bandit Algorithms](https://tor-lattimore.com/downloads/book/book.pdf) (Cambridge, 2020). Free; the reference on bandits.
- **Bertsekas:** Dimitri Bertsekas, [A Course in Reinforcement Learning](https://web.mit.edu/dimitrib/www/RLbook.html) (second edition, 2025). Free; dynamic programming, rollout, and approximation from the control viewpoint.
- **Kochenderfer:** Mykel Kochenderfer, Tim Wheeler, and Kyle Wray, [Algorithms for Decision Making](https://algorithmsbook.com/decisionmaking/) (MIT Press, 2022). Free; exact and approximate MDP methods, online planning, policy search, beliefs and POMDP planning, and multiagent reasoning, with code.
- **Szepesvári:** Csaba Szepesvári, [Algorithms for Reinforcement Learning](https://sites.ualberta.ca/~szepesva/rlbook.html) (Morgan & Claypool, 2010). Free; a concise, rigorous treatment of value prediction and control, with the theory of discounted MDPs.
- **BDR:** Marc Bellemare, Will Dabney, and Mark Rowland, [Distributional Reinforcement Learning](https://distributional-rl.org/) (MIT Press, 2023). Free; the reference on return distributions.
- **MARL:** Stefano Albrecht, Filippos Christianos, and Lukas Schäfer, [Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning: Foundations and Modern Approaches](https://www.marl-book.com/) (MIT Press, 2024). Free; games, solution concepts, and multi-agent deep RL.
- **Tedrake:** Russ Tedrake, [Underactuated Robotics](https://underactuated.mit.edu/). Free course notes; dynamic programming, LQR, trajectory optimization, and policy search for robots.

**Part I — Classical reinforcement learning.** Blocks 1–15 follow Sutton and Barto, Silver, and the DeepMind x UCL lectures, with bandit theory, partial observability, and optimal control added from the supplements.

## <a id="1-markov-decision-processes"></a>1. Markov decision processes

## <a id="labs-and-exercises"></a>Labs and exercises

Every chapter ends with exercises, mixing proofs with experiments on the chapter's code, and each has a worked solution in a collapsed callout. The `Labs` folder holds larger projects, each a page with goals, instructions, expected results, and extensions, and a runnable script in `Labs/code` that reproduces the expected results on a laptop CPU. The labs use [Gymnasium](https://gymnasium.farama.org/) environments and [MinAtar](https://github.com/kenjyoung/MinAtar) ([Young and Tian, 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03176)), miniature versions of five Atari games on a $10\times10$ grid that make deep RL experiments feasible without a GPU.

- Other hands-on material: Sutton and Barto's figures are reproduced in Python in [ShangtongZhang/reinforcement-learning-an-introduction](https://github.com/ShangtongZhang/reinforcement-learning-an-introduction); [CleanRL](https://docs.cleanrl.dev/) gives single-file, benchmarked implementations of DQN, C51, Rainbow, PQN, PPO, DDPG, TD3, SAC, and RND; the [Hugging Face Deep RL course](https://huggingface.co/learn/deep-rl-course/unit0/introduction) has notebooks for Q-learning, DQN, REINFORCE, A2C, PPO, and self-play; the [CS285 homeworks](https://rail.eecs.berkeley.edu/deeprlcourse-fa23/) implement imitation learning, policy gradients, Q-learning and actor-critic, model-based RL, and offline RL; and David Silver's [Easy21 assignment](https://davidstarsilver.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/easy21-assignment.pdf) applies Monte Carlo control, SARSA(λ), and linear function approximation to a card game.
- AI supplies decision theory, filtering, game trees, Monte Carlo tree search, and game theory; DL supplies the networks and optimizers; NLP and LLMs supplies preference learning and reasoning in language models; Generative AI supplies generative policies and the generative models behind world models.
