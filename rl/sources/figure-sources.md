# Figure sources

# <a id="figure-sources"></a>Figure sources

Every figure in the RL chapters is an original plot generated for these notes between 24 and 26 September 2026 with NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib, PyTorch, and Gymnasium; no external artwork or course slides are reproduced. Each chapter's figures come from one script in `Sources/Figure code`, which is the complete record of the construction. The shared module `rlfig.py` sets the palette, fonts, and export (white background, 200 dpi PNG). All environments and data are defined in the scripts: small tabular MDPs (random MDPs, chains, the short corridor, Baird's counterexample, the blind cliffwalk, Deep Sea, and RiverSwim), gridworlds (a world with two goals, cliff walking, the Dyna maze with its blocking and shortcut variants, a constrained gridworld, and random terrain maps), bandits (the 10-armed testbed and Bernoulli, linear contextual, logged-data, and dependent two-armed bandits), random walks of 5, 19, and 1000 states, the gambler's problem, blackjack with an infinite deck, the tiger and heaven-and-hell POMDPs, mountain car, cart-poles and pendulums integrated in NumPy, tic-tac-toe, a biased rock–paper–scissors, toy policies over a few language-model responses, and small continuous tasks (a car on a circular track, a two-dimensional state space with a noisy TV, a deceptive maze, and one-step problems with continuous actions). The one external environment is Gymnasium's `CartPole-v1`, used only by `ch16_dqn.py`; no script imports MuJoCo. SciPy is used by `ch14_pomdp.py` (`linprog`), `ch15_control.py` (`solve_discrete_are`), and `ch17_distributional.py` (`scipy.stats`), and PyTorch by the scripts of chapters 16, 19, 22, 23, 26, and 29. No script loads a file or depends on another apart from `rlfig.py`, and every model is trained from scratch when its script runs; several scripts repeat the setup of code in their chapter, as their docstrings say, but none imports it. The tables below summarize the environments, seeds, and settings of each figure so that it can be read and regenerated without opening the script; the [computing setup](computing-setup.md) gives the environment and commands.

Seeds refer to `numpy.random.default_rng` ("NumPy seed"), `torch.manual_seed` ("torch seed"), or `torch.Generator().manual_seed` ("generator seed"); Gymnasium's `reset(seed=...)` in [chapter 16](../16-deep-q-learning.md) and the generators seeded from Python's `hash` in [chapter 24](../24-planning-with-learned-models.md) are named where they are used. Where a script creates a generator for each run, the runs are identified by their seeds ("NumPy seeds 0–49" means one generator per run); otherwise draws without their own generator, such as exploration, minibatches, and sampled transitions, continue the stream of the script's generator in the order of the script, so a figure's random numbers depend on everything drawn before it in the same script. Figures and panels computed without random draws are marked "no randomness". No script reads command-line arguments; each always writes all its figures. The scripts that use PyTorch set `torch.set_num_threads(1)`, `ch16_dqn.py` before its second figure, the first to use PyTorch, and `ch19_actor_critic.py` also sets `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`, unless it is already set, before importing NumPy. According to their docstrings, `ch01_mdp.py`, `ch12_triad.py`, `ch17_distributional.py`, `ch20_ppo.py`, `ch21_maxent.py`, and `ch27_games.py` take a few seconds, `ch23_models.py`, `ch25_imitation.py`, and `ch28_llm.py` about ten seconds, `ch14_pomdp.py` about fifteen, `ch15_control.py` about twenty, `ch18_rainbow.py` and `ch26_offline.py` about half a minute, `ch16_dqn.py` about a minute and a half, `ch10_planning.py` and `ch22_exploration.py` about two minutes, `ch11_fa.py` about three, and the others (chapters 2–9, 13, 19, 24, and 29–31) about a minute each. Learning curves average the stated number of runs, and routes, paths, and trajectories come from the single run stated; they illustrate the claims in the notes and are not benchmarks.

## <a id="shared-style"></a>Shared style

`rlfig.py` selects Matplotlib's Agg backend, and its `setup()` sets the font DejaVu Sans (10.5 pt, titles 11.5 pt, DejaVu Sans math text), dark axes of line width 0.8 without top and right spines, dark ticks, frameless legends, and white figure, axes, and saved backgrounds. Its `save()` writes each figure to `Sources/Images` as a 200 dpi PNG with a tight bounding box and 0.08 inches of padding, closes it, and prints its name; the scripts of chapters 27–31 pass names without the extension, to which Matplotlib adds `.png`. The palette is BLUE `#2267a5`, ORANGE `#d46a23`, GREEN `#39805a`, PURPLE `#995aa4`, RED `#b32b35`, DARK `#152330` for axes and ticks, GRAY `#586473`, and LIGHT `#c1c8cf`, with light tints of the first five colors defined for fills; the default color cycle is blue, orange, green, purple, red, and gray. Individual scripts set smaller font sizes for titles, annotations, and legends, and use Matplotlib's RdBu, Blues, viridis, and magma colormaps and two-color and three-color colormaps built from the tints.
