# <a id="figure-sources"></a>Figure sources

Every figure in the Generative AI chapters is an original plot generated for these notes on 24 September 2026 with NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib, PyTorch, and scikit-learn; no external artwork or course slides are reproduced. Each chapter's figures come from one script in `Sources/Figure code`, which is the complete record of the construction. The shared module `genfig.py` sets the palette, fonts, and export (white background, 200 dpi PNG). The scripts of chapters 1–3, 7, and 10–12 use scikit-learn's bundled $`8\times8`$ digits (1,797 images with intensities 0–16) with the module's split: a permutation by NumPy `default_rng(0)`, the first 1,500 images for training and the other 297 held out. Chapters 2, 3, and 12 binarize the digits at intensity $`>7`$, and chapters 7, 10, and 11 scale them to $`[-1,1]`$ by $`x/8-1`$. The other figures use synthetic distributions generated in the scripts: two moons, mixtures of Gaussians on a line and on a circle, points on a circle, and demonstrations of a steering task. No script loads a file or depends on another, and every model is trained from scratch when its script runs. [Data sources](data-sources.md) describes the data. The tables below summarize the data, seeds, and model settings of each figure so that it can be read and regenerated without opening the script; the [computing setup](computing-setup.md) gives the environment and commands.

Seeds refer to `torch.manual_seed` ("torch seed"), `torch.Generator().manual_seed` ("generator seed"), or `numpy.random.default_rng` ("NumPy seed"); scikit-learn's `random_state` is named where it is used, and layers whose initialization is not described keep PyTorch's default. Draws without their own generator, such as minibatches, training noise, and fresh samples of synthetic data, continue the stream of the script's torch or NumPy seed in the order of the script. The receptive fields of chapter 2 and the Dirac GAN of chapter 5 involve no randomness. No script reads command-line arguments; each always writes all its figures. The scripts that train with PyTorch set `torch.set_num_threads(1)`, except `ch02_autoregressive.py`, `ch03_vae.py`, and `ch04_flows.py`, which use PyTorch's default; `ch05_gans.py` and `ch08_sde.py` set it before their second figure, the first to use PyTorch. According to their docstrings, `ch01_foundations.py` and `ch13_eval.py` take seconds, `ch04_flows.py`, `ch05_gans.py`, `ch14_policy.py`, and `ch15_memorization.py` about half a minute each, `ch02_autoregressive.py`, `ch09_flow.py`, `ch11_latent.py`, and `ch12_masked.py` about a minute, `ch06_score.py` and `ch08_sde.py` about two minutes, `ch03_vae.py` about three, `ch07_diffusion.py` about four, and `ch10_guidance.py` about six. Training results are single runs; they illustrate the claims in the notes and are not benchmarks.

## <a id="3-variational-autoencoders"></a>3. Variational autoencoders

Script: `ch03_vae.py`. Both figures use VAEs of the binarized digits with the module's split (NumPy seed 0): a diagonal Gaussian encoder with two hidden layers of 256 ReLU units, a decoder of the same shape with Bernoulli logits, and a standard normal prior. Each model is trained for 300 epochs of shuffled minibatches of 100 with Adam at learning rate $`10^{-3}`$ on distortion plus $`\beta`$ times rate, with one reparameterized sample per digit; the models are trained in sequence after one torch seed 0. Rate is the closed-form KL divergence from the prior and distortion the binary cross-entropy of one sample, both reported in bits per image.

## <a id="10-guidance-and-conditional-generation"></a>10. Guidance and conditional generation

Script: `ch10_guidance.py`. Both figures use one class-conditional DDPM of the digits scaled to $`[-1,1]`$ with the module's split (NumPy seed 0), with the linear schedule of chapter 7 ($`T=1000`$, $`\beta_t`$ from $`10^{-4}`$ to 0.02). The noise predictor maps the 64 pixels, the sinusoidal step embedding of chapter 7, and a learned 32-dimensional embedding of the label, with an eleventh entry for no label, through three hidden layers of 512 SiLU units to 64 outputs; during training the label is replaced by the null entry with probability 0.1. Adam with learning rate $`10^{-3}`$, 15,000 steps of 256 digits drawn with replacement, and an exponential moving average of the weights with decay 0.999, which is used for sampling (torch seed 0). The classifier is scikit-learn's logistic regression (`C=0.1`, at most 5,000 iterations) fitted to the training digits.

## <a id="shared-style"></a>Shared style

`genfig.py` selects Matplotlib's Agg backend, and its `setup()` sets the font DejaVu Sans (10.5 pt, titles 11.5 pt, DejaVu Sans math text), dark axes of line width 0.8 without top and right spines, dark ticks, frameless legends, and white figure, axes, and saved backgrounds. Its `save()` writes each figure to `Sources/Images` as a 200 dpi PNG with a tight bounding box and 0.08 inches of padding. The palette is BLUE `#2267a5`, ORANGE `#d46a23`, GREEN `#39805a`, PURPLE `#995aa4`, RED `#b32b35`, DARK `#152330` for axes and ticks, GRAY `#586473`, and LIGHT `#c1c8cf`, with light tints of the first five colors defined for fills; the default color cycle is blue, orange, green, purple, red, and gray. Individual scripts set smaller font sizes for titles, annotations, and legends, and use Matplotlib's gray_r, gray, Blues, RdBu, and tab10 colormaps for images and classes.
