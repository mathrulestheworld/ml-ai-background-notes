# <a id="course-links"></a>Course links

| Course | Links | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UC Berkeley CS188, Spring 2024 — *Introduction to Artificial Intelligence* | [Course](https://inst.eecs.berkeley.edu/~cs188/sp24/) · [Online textbook](https://inst.eecs.berkeley.edu/~cs188/textbook/) · [Lecture playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLp8QV47qJEg67UTShQ4er4RYQ3rOeDKxv) · [Projects](https://inst.eecs.berkeley.edu/~cs188/sp24/projects/) | Main course: agents, search, games, propositional and first-order logic, Bayesian networks, hidden Markov models, particle filtering, and decision networks (chapters 1–2, 4–6, 8–12). Its Pacman projects implement search, adversarial search, logic, and probabilistic tracking with autograders. |
| UC Berkeley CS188, Spring 2023 | [Course](https://inst.eecs.berkeley.edu/~cs188/sp23/) | The two lectures on constraint satisfaction, which Spring 2024 omits (chapter 3). |
| CMU 10-708, Spring 2020 — Eric Xing, *Probabilistic Graphical Models* | [Lectures with slides, scribe notes, and videos](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~epxing/Class/10708-20/lectures.html) | Supplement at graduate depth: undirected models, exact and approximate inference, parameter and structure learning, and causality (chapters 8–11, 13–14). |
| Stanford CS228 — Stefano Ermon, *Probabilistic Graphical Models* | [Course notes](https://ermongroup.github.io/cs228-notes/) | Written notes on representation, inference, and learning (chapters 8–10 and 14). |
| Stanford CS221, Autumn 2019 — Percy Liang and Dorsa Sadigh, *Artificial Intelligence: Principles and Techniques* | [Course](https://stanford-cs221.github.io/autumn2019/) | Selected lectures on factor graphs and logic (chapters 3, 5, 6, and 8). |
| University of Basel, Fall 2023 — Malte Helmert and Gabriele Röger, *Planning and Optimization* | [Course page with slides](https://dmi.unibas.ch/en/studies/computer-science/courses-in-fall-semester-2023/lecture-planning-and-optimization/) | Classical planning in depth: STRIPS, complexity, SAT planning, delete-relaxation heuristics, abstractions, and landmarks (chapter 7). |
| Brady Neal, *Introduction to Causal Inference* | [Course page](https://www.bradyneal.com/causal-inference-course) · [Course book · PDF](https://www.bradyneal.com/Introduction_to_Causal_Inference-Dec17_2020-Neal.pdf) | Potential outcomes, graphs, identification, estimation, instruments, discovery, and counterfactuals (chapter 13). |
| Stanford CS364A, Fall 2013 — Tim Roughgarden, *Algorithmic Game Theory* | [Course page with lecture notes](https://timroughgarden.org/f13/f13.html) | Equilibrium concepts, no-regret dynamics, the price of anarchy, and mechanism design (chapter 15). |
