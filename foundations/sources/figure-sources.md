**Iris data.** R. A. Fisher's [Iris dataset at UCI](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/53/iris), DOI 10.24432/C56C76, is distributed there under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). These plots use the corrected Fisher values supplied by [`load_iris`](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.datasets.load_iris.html). The tree is fitted with scikit-learn's [decision-tree classifier](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/tree.html).

**Digit data.** E. Alpaydin and C. Kaynak, [Optical Recognition of Handwritten Digits (1998), UCI](https://doi.org/10.24432/C50P49), distributed under CC BY 4.0. The figure uses the subset packaged by [`load_digits`](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.datasets.load_digits.html). Values are 8 × 8 block counts derived from digit images. The illustrative split described above is not the original UCI writer-separated benchmark; the data are not MNIST. The MNIST, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet error rates quoted in the chapter are published results, cited there and in [Paper links](paper-links.md); they were not recomputed.

**Old Faithful data.** The `faithful` dataset distributed with R's `datasets` package: 272 eruptions of the Old Faithful geyser, Yellowstone National Park, with eruption length and waiting time to the next eruption in minutes. R's documentation cites W. Härdle, [*Smoothing Techniques: With Implementation in S*](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-4432-5) (Springer, 1991), and refers to the more complete version analyzed by A. Azzalini and A. W. Bowman, [“A Look at Some Data on the Old Faithful Geyser”](https://doi.org/10.2307/2347385), *Applied Statistics* 39 (1990), 357–365. The values were exported unchanged from the `rdatasets` 0.2.10 Python package into `Sources/Figure code/faithful.csv`, keeping the columns `eruptions` and `waiting`.

**Historical references.** [Campbell, Hoane, and Hsu, “Deep Blue,” *Artificial Intelligence* 134 (2002), 57–83](https://research.ibm.com/publications/deep-blue), especially §§2 and 7.3, documents search and evaluation-weight tuning. [LeCun, Bottou, Bengio, and Haffner, “Gradient-Based Learning Applied to Document Recognition,” *Proceedings of the IEEE* 86(11) (1998), 2278–2324](https://bottou.org/papers/lecun-98h), provides the historical convolutional-network example mentioned in the text; no figures from that paper are reproduced here.

## <a id="probability-and-statistics"></a>Probability and statistics

On 26 September 2026 [Probability and Statistics](../04-probability-and-statistics.md) received twenty-nine drawn figures, one for most sections and subsections, and its seven earlier Matplotlib figures were redrawn in the same style under their original file names (marked **Redrawn**). Each figure is drawn from the stated model with D3 and typeset with KaTeX, then rendered to PNG at twice its CSS size by headless Chromium; the source of each is `Sources/Figure code/js/src/<name>.js` (see [Reproducing the figures](#reproducing-the-figures)). Random draws use the seeded linear congruential generator in `js/lib/prob.js`, with Box–Muller Normal draws, so each figure is reproducible exactly. Their individual draws therefore differ from those of the NumPy examples in the chapter; the prediction-interval figure is the exception, and uses the data of its NumPy example. Simulation illustrations do not replace the assumptions or coverage guarantees stated in the notes.

## <a id="reproducing-the-figures"></a>Reproducing the figures

The tables above record the models and sources used for the illustrations. The calculus appendix **Code for optimizer trajectories and simplified plots** reproduces its numerical paths and simplified plots; it does not reproduce every styling detail of the embedded figures. Preserve the stated model, seed, units, and interpretation when regenerating a figure.

The figures added from 25 to 27 September 2026 have complete sources in `Sources/Figure code`:

| Script | Figures and printed values |
| --- | --- |
| `ch01_terminology.py` | The two iris-tree figures, the clear and ambiguous digits, and the Old Faithful densities. It prints the tree's thresholds, errors, and example predictions; the digit probabilities; the held-out accuracies of the network, nearest neighbor, logistic regression, and k-means; the k-means cluster compositions; and the Old Faithful summaries quoted in chapter 1. |
| `foundfig.py` | Shared palette, fonts, and export for `ch01_terminology.py`: opaque white background, 150 dpi PNG cropped to its contents, written to `Sources/Images`. |
| `faithful.csv` | The Old Faithful data described above. |
| `js/src/*.js` | The drawn figures of chapters 2 to 6, one file per figure, named after its PNG. `js/lib/fig.js` and `js/lib/base.css` hold the shared helpers, palette, and fonts; `js/lib/optruns.js` holds the optimizer runs of the calculus Appendix H; and `js/lib/prob.js` holds the seeded random-number generator and the distribution functions of chapter 4. `build.sh` wraps each file into `js/build/<name>.html`, and `render.js` renders it at twice its CSS size and reports any overlapping or clipped labels and any label crossed by a drawn line. |
| `ch06_training_curve.py` | The data of the chapter 6 training-curve figure. It reruns the chapter's training code, records the loss and accuracy of both sets after each epoch and every mini-batch loss, and prints the values embedded in `js/src/numerical-training-curve.js` together with the final values quoted in the chapter. Needs PyTorch and SciPy. |
| `ch03_capture_distill.py` | The four Distill captures, with the changes listed above. Needs network access to distill.pub. |
| `ch03_crop_baydin.py` | The figure from Baydin et al., rendered from the published PDF. |
