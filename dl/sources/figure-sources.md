# Figure sources

# <a id="figure-sources"></a>Figure sources

Every figure in the DL chapters is an original plot generated for these notes on 24 September 2026 with PyTorch 2.2.2 (CPU), NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib, scikit-learn, and NetworkX; no external artwork or course slides are reproduced. Each chapter's figures come from one script in `Sources/Figure code`, which is the complete record of the construction. The shared module `dlfig.py` sets the palette, fonts, and export (opaque white background, 200 dpi PNG). The tables below summarize the data, seeds, and model settings of each figure so that it can be read and regenerated without opening the script; the computing setup gives the environment and commands.

Seeds refer to `torch.manual_seed` ("torch seed"), `torch.Generator().manual_seed` ("generator seed"), or `numpy.random.default_rng` ("NumPy seed") as stated; scikit-learn's `random_state` and NetworkX's `seed` are named where they are used, and layers whose initialization is not described keep PyTorch's default. All scripts call `torch.set_num_threads(1)`. The scripts from chapter 3 on, `ch03_optimization.py` to `ch15_ntk.py`, accept figure numbers as command-line arguments (for example `python ch09_attention.py 5`) and then regenerate only those figures; the numbers follow the order of the figures in the script, which differs from the order in the chapter in chapters 4, 6, and 9, and figure 3 of `ch10_self_supervised.py` writes both the collapse figure and the masked-autoencoder figure. Without arguments a script regenerates all its figures, and `ch01_feedforward.py` and `ch02_initialization.py` always do. According to their docstrings, several scripts take minutes: `ch03_optimization.py` several minutes, `ch08_recurrent.py` about eight for figure 3, `ch09_attention.py` about four for figure 5, `ch10_self_supervised.py` about ten for figure 3, `ch11_scale.py` about two for figure 4, `ch12_methodology.py` about six for figure 3, `ch13_graphs.py` about one for figure 3, `ch14_detection_segmentation.py` about six for figures 2 and 3, and `ch15_ntk.py` about two for figure 1 and ten for figure 2. Training results are single runs unless an average over seeds is stated; they illustrate the claims in the notes and are not benchmarks.

## <a id="shared-style"></a>Shared style

`dlfig.py` selects Matplotlib's Agg backend, and its `setup()` sets the font DejaVu Sans (10.5 pt, titles 11.5 pt, DejaVu Sans math text), dark axes without top and right spines, frameless legends, and white figure, axes, and saved backgrounds. Its `save()` writes each figure to `Sources/Images` as a 200 dpi PNG with a tight bounding box. The palette is BLUE `#2267a5`, ORANGE `#d46a23`, GREEN `#39805a`, PURPLE `#995aa4`, RED `#b32b35`, DARK `#152330` for axes and ticks, GRAY `#586473`, and LIGHT `#c1c8cf`, with light tints of the first five colors for fills; the default color cycle is blue, orange, green, purple, red, and gray.
